# Business Problem

## The company

Hearth & Desk Supply Co. is a fictional maker of office furniture accessories in Pompano Beach, FL: desk organizers, monitor stands, cable trays and laptop risers. It buys 11 components from 5 vendors, builds 4 finished goods against bills of materials and ships to 7 business customers across 7 states.

## The question

When a vendor runs late, does the slip reach the customer, and do the books still match at close?

Answering that takes four records that live in different parts of the business: purchase orders, receipts, manufacturing orders and deliveries, plus the vendor bills behind them. Each one can be right on its own while the chain between them breaks.

## What was measured

- **Customers:** did every order ship complete and on or before its promised date (OTIF), and how long did it take from confirmation to shipment?
- **Vendors:** did each first receipt arrive by the confirmed date, and how much of the order came on that first truck?
- **Production:** did each manufacturing order finish by its milestone baseline?
- **Shipping:** what did each order cost to ship, and did the carrier choice make sense for the destination and weight?
- **Project:** how far did each milestone land from its baseline?

## Scenarios built in

- Greenline Bamboo Supply, the single source for bamboo panels and shelf boards, has the longest lead time. It was set up to miss its date and ship a partial.
- Coastal Express only serves four southeastern states, so out-of-region orders need a carrier decision.
- Vendor lead times were compressed to 1 to 5 days so every receipt landed inside the 14-day trial.

The disruptions were planned. The responses, corrections and the one issue nobody planned (a received PO that was never billed) happened in the system as the work ran.
